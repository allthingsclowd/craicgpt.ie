I remember the road more than anything else. It was just another stretch of dusty highway in Afghanistan, our convoy moving slowly between villages as the morning heat started to build. We'd driven that route before, and after a while you start to believe routine means safety. Then everything changed in a second. The crack of gunfire echoed across the valley and the radio burst into life with shouted reports. Training took over before fear had a chance to. We stayed focused, checking on each other and following our drills while the vehicles pushed through the contact area. Looking back, what stays with me isn't the noise or the confusion—it's the faces of the men beside me. In moments like that, you realise how much you trust the people around you, and how quickly an ordinary day can become one you'll never forget
